## Context

FleetFidelity, a Yahara Software product, had just validated its fleet management tool to several customers. The goal was simple: build something that connects the myriad of tools fleet managers use to track truck drivers and deliveries.

The next step? Build a go-to-market marketing website that showcases FleetFidelity's value.

The timeline was narrow, about six weeks end-to-end.

## Goals

FleetFidelity was aiming to better clarify its product story, establish a standalone brand, and lay the foundation for future growth and strategy.

At the start, FleetFidelity had a website deeply nested within its parent company's website, which was difficult to find and fairly basic in structure. It was useful for tradeshows, but not so much for investors.

## ![Old website, part 1](/uploads/ff-prev-1.png)![Old website, part 2](/uploads/ff-prev-2.png)Process

Early on, we defined goals and scope in a clear brief in a four page, brand-focused website.

The first step included visual mockups in Figma, along with an expanded styleguide. From the beginning, I took what was used well and expanded it while keeping visuals unified.

Given the timeline, I opted to leverage open source visuals in the form of [Lucide](https://lucide.dev/) icons and [Streamline](https://www.streamlinehq.com/) illustrations.

![Branding splash](/uploads/ff-og-sample.png)![Typography and color palette](/uploads/styleguide.png)![Lucide and Streamline icons](/uploads/icons.png)![High-resolution Figma mockups](/uploads/mockups.png)After feedback and a few revisions, I got to work planning the development of the website using [Next JS](https://nextjs.org/), [Tailwind](https://tailwindcss.com/), and [Tina](https://tina.io/) as the CMS.

Before beginning development, I created a high-level content model supporting [block-based](https://tina.io/blog/what-are-blocks) content.

Each section of the page is fully re-usable and customizable for website authors.![FleetFidelity content model](/uploads/ff-content-model.png "FleetFidelity content model")Each bold label on the left corresponds to a "collection" of content in the CMS.

To make the CMS as flexible as possible, I configured navigation to be plug-and-play. An author can create a new page, link to it in the navigation/footer, then begin adding blocks to the final page (not necessarily in that order).

The end result is a mobile-friendly, highly customizable CMS-powered marketing website. In the future, they're in a great position to add a blog and other expert-crafted content.
